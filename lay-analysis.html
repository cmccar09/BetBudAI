1

Add a minimum odds floor of 2.0 — stop laying very short-priced favourites

The 1.5–2.0 odds band loses money at every score tier. At 13+ score, 63 races in this band returned −7.76 pts (38.1% win rate vs 42.9% needed). Very short-priced favourites win too often — the market is right about them. Add fav_odds ≥ 2.0 as a hard gate before any lay signal is shown. This removes the worst-value selections without reducing the genuinely profitable 2.0–2.5 band.

Est. saving +7–10 pts/period Effort 30 min code change
2

Add a score-gap filter — only lay when our system strongly disagrees with the market

The lay signal is most powerful when our top-scoring horse scores significantly higher than the market favourite. Require the favourite's system score to be at least 8 points below the top scorer's (score_gap ≥ 8). This filters out races where the favourite is also a strong scorer — those are the races where the favourite wins despite a high lay score on another horse. Track score_gap in the CSV and run a 30-day analysis after 2–3 weeks of data collection.

Est. impact Reduces false signals by ~20% Effort 1–2 hrs + 3 weeks data
3

Track going type and class per lay result — identify where favourites are most vulnerable

Favourites get beaten more often in certain conditions: soft/heavy going (ground specialists outrun their odds), large fields (traffic problems), and lower-class handicaps (open form books). The current settlement data does not capture going or class per race. Add going and race_class fields to each races_settled entry in the DynamoDB snapshot. After 4 weeks, run a breakdown to see if win rates differ significantly by condition — if they do, restrict VIP Elite to the profitable going/class subsets only.

Est. impact Unknown until data collected Effort 2 hrs code + 4 weeks data
4

Track odds movement — only lay drifting favourites, not steamers

A favourite whose odds are shortening from morning to race time (steaming) means informed money is backing it — that favourite is more likely to win. A favourite whose odds are drifting (lengthening) is being opposed by the market. The Betfair pipeline already captures opening and current odds. Add a fav_price_drift_pct field: only trigger a lay when the favourite has drifted ≥5% from its morning price. Steamers should be excluded entirely from lay signals.

Est. impact Removes ~15% of losing signals Effort 2–3 hrs + 4 weeks data
5

Never increase volume by loosening criteria — fewer selections at higher confidence wins

The current problem started because the selection threshold was 4+ score, which selected every race. The profitable filter produces 1–3 selections per day. That is the right volume. Do not be tempted to loosen score, odds, or gap thresholds to increase daily selection count — the data shows clearly that every loosening step reduces ROI. If a day has zero qualifying selections, show zero. Subscribers get better outcomes from three high-quality alerts per week than from daily low-confidence ones.

Impact Protects ROI long-term Effort Process discipline only

Target state — what profitable looks like

With steps 1–4 in place: lay_score ≥ 13 AND fav_odds 2.0–2.5 AND score_gap ≥ 8 AND fav_drifting = true. Historical base (score 13+ / 2.0–2.5) already delivers +7.7% ROI over 80 races. Each additional filter should push win rate from 60% toward 65%+, which at 2.25 avg odds translates to approximately +12–15% ROI. At 1–3 selections per day, that is a genuinely profitable, defensible lay product for Favourite Watch subscribers.
